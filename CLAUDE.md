# Smokey's Party Bus website

Marketing site + lead capture for Smokey's, a black party bus brand. Plan and decisions: `docs/architecture.md`.

## Stack
Next.js 16.4 (App Router, all routes static) · React 19.3 · TypeScript 6 strict (typescript-eslint caps TS < 6.1) · Tailwind 4 (tokens in `src/app/globals.css`) · Motion 13 for normal UI (`motion/react`, `m` components under `LazyMotion` in `src/components/providers.tsx`) · GSAP 3 for the intro only · the living sky behind every page is a small hand-written WebGL shader plus DOM layers (no Three.js).

## Local tooling
Node 24 lives at `~/.local/node/bin` (not on the default PATH). Prefix commands:
`export PATH=~/.local/node/bin:$PATH` then `npm run dev | build | lint | typecheck`.

## Conventions
- Content lives in `src/content/*.ts`. Anything unconfirmed has `sample: true` / `placeholder: true` and renders a visible `<SampleTag>`. Never present placeholder art or specs as the real bus.
- All media goes through `src/content/assets.ts` slots; `src: null` renders the illustrated placeholder.
- The intro film is the owner's own AI render. It carries the generator's small "Ai" badge (top left of the frame): visible on desktop and partly on phones with a toolbar; never removed without the owner's say-so. Its bus is a generic coach, not Smokey's: keep `introFilm.placeholder` (the "Placeholder footage" tag, shown in every layout) until EVERY built cut shows the real bus. Never add Warner Bros. material (the cartoon roadrunner, its name, sounds or artwork) and never use third-party footage (e.g. TikTok downloads) in the deployed site: the owner provides licensed or own footage.
- Server Components by default; `"use client"` only on interactive leaves. The tunnel scene's layers are server-rendered slots passed into the client `IntroSequence`; each scene of the world is a Server Component in `src/components/scenes/`.
- Motion values come from `src/lib/motion.ts` (mirrors CSS tokens). Honour reduced motion: `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`; the tunnel engine has its own stills-only timeline; the world's easing, entrances and the guide bus's bob switch off.
- Nav links are the world's scenes as homepage anchors ("/#bus", so they work from any route; in the world they glide to the scene): `src/content/site.ts`. Informational content lives IN the world; only ACTIONS open something over it: `data-action="book|quote|schedule|message|email"` opens the booking panel (`src/components/actions/action-drawer.tsx`; optional `data-book-package` / `data-book-occasion` presets), calls are `tel:` links. Without JS those links are plain `/#contact` links. The panel only composes a text or email for the visitor to send (`src/lib/booking-message.ts`): the site sends nothing and books nothing.
- Two themes, switched by the sky: `html[data-sky="day"]` (white glass, deep navy text) and night (navy glass, near-white text; also the no-JS default). Components use only the tokens (`bg-surface`, `bg-surface-raised`, `bg-muted`, `ring-border`, `text-muted-foreground`, `text-gold` for gold TEXT, `bg-primary` for gold fills, `bg-ink` for black-and-gold): never `white/x`, `black` or neon in UI (the neons are only for the party illustrations). Anything that always sits on dark media (the intro stage, the gallery lightbox) gets `.theme-night`. Sections are glass panels (`glass` utility + `data-panel`) floating over the sky; no section paints a solid background. The gallery lightbox shows each photo whole (`object-contain` on a stage sized by the screen, never by the photo): `npm run test:gallery`.

## The world (src/components/world)
The homepage is ONE continuous world, NOT an intro followed by a page: the tunnel scene, then every scene of the site, side by side, then the footer. Never add anything that makes the tunnel feel like a pre-roll (a loader reset, a fade to a "homepage", a separate hero, a "skip intro").
- Axis: sideways (`html[data-axis="x"]`, set before first paint by the boot script in `layout.tsx` and kept up to date by `World`) wherever the screen is at least 40rem tall; below that (a phone on its side, 400% zoom: WCAG 1.4.10) and without JS, the same scenes stack as a column and the page scrolls down. Style sideways-only changes with the `world:` variant (globals.css) so the column stays as it is.
- `[data-world]` is a fixed, full-screen horizontal scroll container (NOT the page: a page wider than the screen makes phones zoom out and report the wrong width). Touch swipes, trackpad swipes, focus and `#links` move it natively; `world-scroll.ts` adds the wheel (stepped wheels eased, trackpads 1:1; a panel that can still scroll vertically goes first), mouse drag, scene keys (PageDown/Space…) and bringing a partly visible focused element fully into view; `world.tsx` glides in-page links to the START of their scene (pushState, focus), restores the visitor's place (sessionStorage) and tracks the scene in view for the header nav (`aria-current="location"`).
- Scenes: `<Scene>` (`scene.tsx`): a glass panel between the header and the road, the screen's width or, with `wide`, as wide as its content (columns ALONG the world, explicit widths). Every panel must fit its screen without inner vertical scrolling (the panel's own scroll is only a safety net): lay tall content out sideways, never stack it. `npm run test:world` measures this at desktop and phone sizes.
- Connecting it: the living sky (clouds drift sideways with the world), one continuous road along the bottom (the world's `local` background, carrying on from the film's road), the guide bus (`guide-bus.tsx`: a generic decorative silhouette, never presented as Smokey's bus) on it once the tunnel leaves, the film's dark carrying on into the sky as its stage moves off, and each panel gliding in as it arrives (scroll-driven CSS, off the main thread).

## The tunnel scene (src/components/intro)
The first scene of the world, a film in one pinned stage: ONE continuous shot scrubbed by the scroll. The roadrunner runs at the tunnel → runs in, the light goes and a black closes over everything → complete darkness (the film JUMPS, unseen, from its `dark` beat to its `lights` beat: the footage's own lit-dust swap and the bus waiting in the tunnel are never shown) → two headlights glow through the dark → the bus's shape → it drives out onto the road → SMOKEY'S and the CTAs appear in the scene → the camera drifts on and the stage moves off as the world carries on. Scrolling back plays it in reverse. The header is visible throughout.
- The FILM provides all the movement (`src/content/intro-video.ts`: the one place for its processing, framing and story beats, merged with the build's `intro-video.generated.json` in `intro-film.ts`). Two cuts of the same shot, each from its own master: `portrait` (phones, tall screens) and `wide` (16:9: desktops, landscape). Each screen gets the closer cut; while one has no master the other stands in (a wide screen shows the portrait cut as a feathered band over a pre-blurred glow of the same scene). Web files in `public/video/intro/` are BUILT, never hand-made: masters in `assets/intro-source/video/` (git-ignored; see its README) → `npm run video:build`. Their names carry a hash of their bytes (`film-portrait.<hash>.mp4`: `scripts/intro-video/hash.mjs`, the build's last step), recorded in the generated JSON and served `immutable` (`next.config.ts`): never rename one by hand or reference one by name; `npm run video:hash` re-names existing files without re-encoding. A new render only needs its own `beats` (tunnel, dark, swallowed, cover, headlights, lights, rest: seconds after trim.start; `dark` and `lights` are the darkest frames either side of the swap, where the black hides the jump; read off `npm run video:inspect -- <master> --from= --to= --step=` strips; the build ends the film just after `rest`).
- `intro-engine.ts`: ONE GSAP master timeline, scrubbed by ScrollTrigger over a long section (`.intro-scroll`: wide in the sideways world, `scroller: [data-world], horizontal: true`; tall in the column) whose stage is pinned with CSS sticky (left:0 / top:0). Timeline units = scroll, not seconds (`intro-config.ts`): each stretch between the film's beats gets its own share of the scroll; the black (`INTRO.darkness`) is a `[data-fx="dark"]` layer. The film NEVER plays: it's seeked frame-accurately to the playhead (encoded at ≤30 fps, a keyframe every 4 frames, no B-frames), one seek in flight, so autoplay rules don't apply. GSAP adds the camera (a push, a charge into the tunnel, settling on the bus, a drift sideways at the end), the black, the glow crossfades, the caption, branding and CTAs. Film and header state are derived in one idempotent `sync()` from the playhead, so any scroll jump in either direction lands on the right frame; sync() ignores ScrollTrigger's revert during a refresh and re-runs after it.
- Scrubbed-timeline rules: every tween is a fromTo with explicit start AND end, later tweens use `immediateRender: false`, and each element's CSS resting state equals its first tween's start (ScrollTrigger reverts the timeline when it re-measures). Never call ScrollTrigger.refresh() while the page is scrolling (it restores the scroll position it measured at).
- `intro-media.ts`: picks the cut and file for the screen (phone-sized → the lighter `-small` file; H.264 first, VP9 next), locks in that cut's framing, stills and glows (turning a phone never swaps them), downloads the film COMPLETELY to a blob URL and primes its decoder before the scroll unlocks (no stalls or black frames). `intro-sequence.tsx`: life cycle only (branded loader until the opening still is on screen, then "Loading the ride…" with progress while the world waits at its start; the engine restarts if the world changes axis), zero React renders while scrolling. If the film isn't in memory within `INTRO.filmTimeoutMs`, the stills tell the story; a slow connection gets them without that wait (`INTRO.slowNet`: at once on 2G, or once the download, a couple of seconds in, is on course to take too long; it's then cancelled).
- Framing: the film sits in `.intro-film` (globals.css) with its cut's own aspect ratio: covers the frame (the small viewport; the stage itself is 100lvh so the film fills a phone once its toolbar hides) like object-fit cover, never cropping more than `1 − minVisible` of its width or height; past that it becomes a feathered band (`data-banded` y/x) over the glow. Tall/wide framing is chosen in CSS (first paint is right without JS). Never stretch footage.
- 60fps rules: transforms and opacity only; nothing created mid-scroll; the glows are pre-blurred at build time (no live CSS blur). Check with `npm run perf:intro -- --gpu --frames` (page fps and film frames shown per second) and `npm run test:intro` (exits 1 on any uncaught page error or failed check: the film where it should play, the stills early on a slow connection, the film's files cached for good) against a production build.
- Accessibility: reduced motion, Save-Data or 2G = the same story as crossfaded stills (start, the black, rest) on a shorter scroll, no film downloaded. The H1 stays in the accessibility tree throughout (opacity only); the CTAs are invisible (out of the Tab order) until they appear. The header's nav (Home = the branding, `#intro-end`) and the "Skip to content" link jump past the scene. No JavaScript (or the engine fails): the finished hero on the film's resting frame, no scroll story.

## Living sky (src/components/sky)
Behind every page (`LivingSky` in the root layout; `body` paints no background, `html` does, under it): day (blue sky, sun with soft rays, white clouds) or night (navy sky, detailed moon with a halo, twinkling stars, a rare shooting star). It follows the visitor's day/night booking preference, or the local time of day until they choose (the pre-paint `skyBootScript` in `layout.tsx` sets `data-sky` first: no flash).
- ONE number drives it all: the phase, 0 = day … 1 = night (`sky-engine.ts`). A change animates it over `SKY.transitionMs` (day → sunset → twilight → night, or back as a sunrise); every layer reads it. Colours and tunables: `src/content/sky.ts` (keyframes mixed in OKLab, `sky-color.ts`).
- Cost rules: during a change, only OPACITY animates in the DOM (the four gradient keyframes, the sun's white and warm versions, the moon's halo and the stars are stacked static layers crossfaded on the compositor); per-frame CSS variables go only to the sky layer and `[data-sky-follow]` elements, NEVER to html (that restyles the whole page every frame). The page's theme flips once, half-way, inside a View Transition (a GPU crossfade of two snapshots; instant without it or with reduced motion). The clouds (`sky-clouds.ts`: 3 parallax layers of domain-warped noise, lit from the sun/moon) are the only per-frame drawing: a fraction of the resolution, capped at `SKY.cloudMaxPixels` (big screens never cost more), 30 fps when idle, full rate while scrolling or changing, nothing while the tunnel scene covers the sky (IntersectionObserver on `#main`) or the tab is hidden; sideways through the world the clouds drift sideways (the shader's `uScroll` is [x, y]). Stars and the moon are painted once (`sky-paint.ts`; the stars only when night first arrives). No WebGL, no high-precision floats or a lost context: the gradient sky without clouds, never an error. Reduced motion: the sun and moon stay put (they fade, never travel), no drift, parallax, twinkle or shooting stars; a short change.
- Choosing: clicking the sun or the moon on the bare sky (never through content: the engine's hit test skips anything inside panels, text, media or controls), the header's compact switch (in the phone menu drawer below `sm`), or the "Day or night?" section's picker (`PeriodControl`, a real radiogroup). All write `period` to `src/lib/booking-store.ts`: ONE merged record (`smokeys:booking` in localStorage) that the booking form will extend; a change never erases other fields. The booking panel and the Contact scene pre-fill the text/email with it and everything else the visitor filled in. A preference is never availability: the visitor still picks the date and time.
- The bus photo's panel follows the sky (`data-sky-follow`): its own sky gradient, warm sunlight on the bus by day, a night grade with the headlights and roof markers glowing at night (masked to the cut-out's outline; light positions in `BusLight`, measured on `src/assets/intro/bus-exterior.webp`).
- Check with `npm run test:sky` (13 checks: what's drawn through each transition, clicking the sky, the booking merge, reduced motion, measured contrast day and night, frame rate, no-JS, and a "Meet the bus" hotspot tapped on a phone; `--quick` skips the 26 s shooting-star wait) against a production build.

## Intro assets pipeline
Despite the name, these are the bus photos ("Meet the bus", via `src/content/assets.ts`), not the intro: the film has its own pipeline (`npm run video:build`). Owner originals live untouched in `assets/intro-source/`. `npm run assets:intro` (`scripts/intro-assets/build.mjs`; macOS 14+, needs `swiftc`) regenerates the two images in `src/assets/intro/`: `bus-exterior.webp` (Apple Vision subject cutout, street removed, 2x Lanczos from the 560px source) and `bus-interior.jpg` (copied untouched, or uprighted and metadata-stripped if it's a camera photo). Replace a source file and re-run; never hand-edit generated files. If a replacement photo changes framing, re-measure the hotspot positions in `src/content/bus.ts` and the night light positions (headlights, roof markers) in `BusLight` (`src/components/home/meet-the-bus.tsx`).
