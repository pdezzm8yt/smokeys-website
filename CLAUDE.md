# Roadrunning Party Bus Rental Service website

Marketing site + lead capture for Roadrunning Party Bus Rental Service ("Roadrunning" for short: `site.name`; the full name is `site.legalName`, in `src/content/site.ts`), a black party bus brand. (It was called Smokey's until November 2026: internal keys like `smokeys:booking` keep the old name so visitors' saved details survive.) Plan and decisions: `docs/architecture.md`.

## Stack
Next.js 16.4 (App Router, all routes static) · React 19.3 · TypeScript 6 strict (typescript-eslint caps TS < 6.1) · Tailwind 4 (tokens in `src/app/globals.css`) · Motion 13 for normal UI (`motion/react`, `m` components under `LazyMotion` in `src/components/providers.tsx`) · GSAP 3 for the intro only · the living sky behind every page is a small hand-written WebGL shader plus DOM layers (no Three.js).

## Local tooling
Node 24 lives at `~/.local/node/bin` (not on the default PATH). Prefix commands:
`export PATH=~/.local/node/bin:$PATH` then `npm run dev | build | lint | typecheck`.

## Conventions
- Content lives in `src/content/*.ts`. Anything unconfirmed has `sample: true` / `placeholder: true` and renders a visible `<SampleTag>`. Never present placeholder art or specs as the real bus.
- All media goes through `src/content/assets.ts` slots; `src: null` renders the illustrated placeholder.
- The intro film is the owner's own AI render. It carries the generator's small "Ai" badge (top left of the frame): visible on desktop and partly on phones with a toolbar; never removed without the owner's say-so. Its bus is a generic coach, not Roadrunning's own: keep `introFilm.placeholder` (the "Placeholder footage" tag, shown in every layout) until EVERY built cut shows the real bus. Never add Warner Bros. material (the cartoon roadrunner, its name, sounds or artwork) and never use third-party footage (e.g. TikTok downloads) in the deployed site: the owner provides licensed or own footage.
- Server Components by default; `"use client"` only on interactive leaves. The tunnel scene's layers are server-rendered slots passed into the client `IntroSequence`; each scene of the world is a Server Component in `src/components/scenes/`.
- Motion values come from `src/lib/motion.ts` (mirrors CSS tokens). Honour reduced motion: `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`; the tunnel engine has its own stills-only timeline; the world's easing, entrances and the guide bus's bob switch off.
- Nav links are the world's scenes as homepage anchors ("/#bus", so they work from any route; in the world they travel to the scene, ahead, with no URL or history change): `src/content/site.ts`. Informational content lives IN the world; only ACTIONS open something over it: `data-action="book|quote|schedule|message|email"` opens the booking panel (`src/components/actions/action-drawer.tsx`; optional `data-book-package` / `data-book-occasion` presets), calls are `tel:` links. Without JS those links are plain `/#contact` links. The panel only composes a text or email for the visitor to send (`src/lib/booking-message.ts`; on a computer also a QR code of the text, scanned to send it from their phone: `text-from-phone.tsx`, its payload shortened to 420 UTF-8 bytes by whole characters, never the other details): the site sends nothing and books nothing.
- Two themes, switched by the sky: `html[data-sky="day"]` (white glass, deep navy text) and night (navy glass, near-white text; also the no-JS default). Components use only the tokens (`bg-surface`, `bg-surface-raised`, `bg-muted`, `ring-border`, `text-muted-foreground`, `text-gold` for gold TEXT, `bg-primary` for gold fills, `bg-ink` for black-and-gold): never `white/x`, `black` or neon in UI (the neons are only for the party illustrations). Anything that always sits on dark media (the intro stage, the gallery lightbox) gets `.theme-night`. Sections are glass panels (`glass` utility + `data-panel`) floating over the sky; no section paints a solid background. The gallery lightbox shows each photo whole (`object-contain` on a stage sized by the screen, never by the photo): `npm run test:gallery`.

## The world (src/components/world)
The homepage is ONE continuous world, NOT an intro followed by a page: the tunnel scene, then every scene of the site, side by side, then the footer, and then (sideways) straight back round to the tunnel: a LOOP that only goes forward. Never add anything that makes the tunnel feel like a pre-roll (a loader reset, a fade to a "homepage", a separate hero, a "skip intro"), and never a visible end or reset to the loop (no snap back, no fade or flash at the join, no rewinding).
- Axis: sideways (`html[data-axis="x"]`, set before first paint by the boot script in `layout.tsx` and kept up to date by `World`) wherever the screen is at least 40rem tall; below that (a phone on its side, 400% zoom: WCAG 1.4.10) and without JS, the same scenes stack as a column and the page scrolls down. Style sideways-only changes with the `world:` variant (globals.css) so the column stays as it is.
- `[data-world]` is a fixed, full-screen horizontal scroll container (NOT the page: a page wider than the screen makes phones zoom out and report the wrong width), a native scroller (composited; focus, find-in-page and assistive tech still move it, and everything follows), but the visitor's own input is all `world-scroll.ts`'s: touch-action `pinch-zoom` on the world and everything in it once `data-world-ready` (a panel whose content overflows, `data-overflows`, keeps vertical pans), a non-passive wheel, keys, mouse drag. FORWARD ONLY: wheel (stepped eased, trackpad 1:1; a panel that can still scroll vertically goes first), swipes (sideways, or up), drags with a throw, →/↓/PageDown/Space, the "Next stop" button (WCAG 2.5.1/2.5.7: a tap for every swipe) all move it on; anything asking to go BACK only tugs it a little (≤ 8% of the screen) and it springs forward again (nothing at all with reduced motion). Home goes to `#intro-end`, End to the footer, as nav links do. `world.tsx` sends in-page links to the START of their scene the NEXT time the journey gets there (back only to re-align within the scene the visitor is in, or one a screen behind), with no URL or history change (a deep link's hash is dropped once applied), restores the visitor's place (sessionStorage) and tracks the scene in view for the header nav (`aria-current="location"`).
- The loop: one lap = the world's children end to end. Once the end of the lap is in sight (the footer coming in, the tunnel off the screen either way), the tunnel scene is moved round to the END of the world (CSS `order`, `#top[data-round]`, scrollLeft down by its width in the same task: nothing on screen moves; the road's dashes are kept in place with `--road-shift`); the journey then travels into the REAL tunnel, its stage arriving from the right (led by its own edge in mirror image, `OpeningLead`) and docking pinned; there, pinned and covering the screen (identical either way), it goes back to the start and a new lap begins. No copies of anything. `world-axis.ts` keeps the journey (`worldTravel()`: laps + shift + scrollLeft) so nothing that moves with it jumps, and every move (eases, trips, throws, drags) is made along the journey, so velocity carries straight round. Trips past the tunnel (`html[data-trip="past"]`) leave its stage as it is and pass its pinned stretch in one frame (it looks the same all the way): a nav click never replays the story; trips into it (Home) play it at a steady pace. `html[data-loop-hold]` (the tunnel's engine not running yet) holds the journey at the join. Check the join, the shift, continuity across it and the trips with `npm run test:world` (pixel diffs at the join and the shift, per-frame travel with one wrap and no step back).
- Scenes: `<Scene>` (`scene.tsx`): a glass panel between the header and the road, the screen's width or, with `wide`, as wide as its content (columns ALONG the world, explicit widths). Every panel must fit its screen without inner vertical scrolling (the panel's own scroll is only a safety net): lay tall content out sideways, never stack it. `npm run test:world` measures this at desktop and phone sizes.
- Connecting it: the living sky (clouds drift sideways with the world), one continuous road along the bottom (the world's `local` background, carrying on from the film's road), the guide bus (`guide-bus.tsx`: a generic decorative silhouette, never presented as Roadrunning's own bus) on it once the tunnel leaves and driving off ahead into the tunnel's light when the loop brings it back round, the film's dark carrying on into the sky as its stage moves off (and its own light leading it back in), a "you are here" mark going round the loop along the bottom, and each panel gliding in as it arrives (scroll-driven CSS, off the main thread).

## The tunnel scene (src/components/intro)
The first scene of the world, a film in one pinned stage: ONE continuous shot scrubbed by the scroll. The roadrunner runs at the tunnel → runs in, the light goes and a black closes over everything → complete darkness (the film JUMPS, unseen, from its `dark` beat to its `lights` beat: the footage's own lit-dust swap and the bus waiting in the tunnel are never shown) → two headlights glow through the dark → the bus's shape → it drives out onto the road → ROADRUNNING (PARTY BUS RENTAL SERVICE) and the CTAs appear in the scene → the camera drifts on and the stage moves off as the world carries on. The story only plays forward as the visitor travels into it (each time round the loop); a jump back (focus, find) shows whatever frame that place has. The header is visible throughout.
- The FILM provides all the movement (`src/content/intro-video.ts`: the one place for its processing, framing and story beats, merged with the build's `intro-video.generated.json` in `intro-film.ts`). Two cuts of the same shot, each from its own master: `portrait` (phones, tall screens) and `wide` (16:9: desktops, landscape). Each screen gets the closer cut; while one has no master the other stands in (a wide screen shows the portrait cut as a feathered band over a pre-blurred glow of the same scene). Web files in `public/video/intro/` are BUILT, never hand-made: masters in `assets/intro-source/video/` (git-ignored; see its README) → `npm run video:build`. Their names carry a hash of their bytes (`film-portrait.<hash>.mp4`: `scripts/intro-video/hash.mjs`, the build's last step), recorded in the generated JSON and served `immutable` (`next.config.ts`): never rename one by hand or reference one by name; `npm run video:hash` re-names existing files without re-encoding. A new render only needs its own `beats` (tunnel, dark, swallowed, cover, headlights, lights, rest: seconds after trim.start; `dark` and `lights` are the darkest frames either side of the swap, where the black hides the jump; read off `npm run video:inspect -- <master> --from= --to= --step=` strips; the build ends the film just after `rest`).
- `intro-engine.ts`: ONE GSAP master timeline, scrubbed by the scroll over a long section (`.intro-scroll`: wide in the sideways world, tall in the column) whose stage is pinned with CSS sticky (left:0 / top:0). No ScrollTrigger: its own driver maps the section's progress (range = section − stage, so a phone's toolbar never changes it) and eases the timeline after it with `gsap.quickTo(totalProgress, { duration: INTRO.scroll.scrub, ease: "expo" })` (ScrollTrigger's own scrub mechanism); off the screen (behind the visitor: finished; ahead, round at the world's end: its start) it's set at once, the film re-parked out of sight (`video[data-parking]`) until it shows the new frame. During a world trip past it (`data-trip="past"`) its on-screen stage is left as it is; on a trip into it (`"story"`) it follows the trip without easing. Timeline units = scroll, not seconds (`intro-config.ts`): each stretch between the film's beats gets its own share of the scroll; the black (`INTRO.darkness`) is a `[data-fx="dark"]` layer. The film NEVER plays: it's seeked frame-accurately to the playhead (encoded at ≤30 fps, a keyframe every 4 frames, no B-frames), one seek in flight, so autoplay rules don't apply. GSAP adds the camera (a push, a charge into the tunnel, settling on the bus, a drift sideways at the end), the black, the glow crossfades, the caption, branding and CTAs. Film and header state are derived in one idempotent `sync()` from the playhead, so any jump in either direction lands on the right frame.
- Scrubbed-timeline rules: every tween is a fromTo with explicit start AND end, later tweens use `immediateRender: false`, and each element's CSS resting state equals its first tween's start (any position, reached from any direction or after any jump, looks the same). GSAP never animates an element that CSS centres with a translate (it folds the translate into its own transform, rounded, and can lose it): animate an inner wrapper (the scroll hint does).
- `intro-media.ts`: picks the cut and file for the screen (phone-sized → the lighter `-small` file; H.264 first, VP9 next), locks in that cut's framing, stills and glows (turning a phone never swaps them), downloads the film COMPLETELY to a blob URL and primes its decoder before the scroll unlocks (no stalls or black frames). `intro-sequence.tsx`: life cycle only (branded loader until the opening still is on screen, then "Loading the ride…" with progress while the world waits at its start; the engine restarts if the world changes axis), zero React renders while scrolling. If the film isn't in memory within `INTRO.filmTimeoutMs`, the stills tell the story; a slow connection gets them without that wait (`INTRO.slowNet`: at once on 2G, or once the download, a couple of seconds in, is on course to take too long; it's then cancelled).
- Framing: the film sits in `.intro-film` (globals.css) with its cut's own aspect ratio: covers the frame (the small viewport; the stage itself is 100lvh so the film fills a phone once its toolbar hides) like object-fit cover, never cropping more than `1 − minVisible` of its width or height; past that it becomes a feathered band (`data-banded` y/x) over the glow. Tall/wide framing is chosen in CSS (first paint is right without JS). Never stretch footage.
- 60fps rules: transforms and opacity only; nothing created mid-scroll; the glows are pre-blurred at build time (no live CSS blur). Check with `npm run perf:intro -- --gpu --frames` (page fps and film frames shown per second) and `npm run test:intro` (exits 1 on any uncaught page error or failed check: the film where it should play, the stills early on a slow connection, the film's files cached for good) against a production build.
- Accessibility: reduced motion, Save-Data or 2G = the same story as crossfaded stills (start, the black, rest) on a shorter scroll, no film downloaded. The H1 stays in the accessibility tree throughout (opacity only); the CTAs are invisible (out of the Tab order) until they appear. The header's nav (Home = the branding, `#intro-end`) and the "Skip to content" link get past the scene (Skip from inside it; elsewhere it just moves focus to the scene in view). No JavaScript (or the engine fails): the finished hero on the film's resting frame, no scroll story.

## Living sky (src/components/sky)
Behind every page (`LivingSky` in the root layout; `body` paints no background, `html` does, under it): day (blue sky, sun with soft rays, white clouds) or night (navy sky, detailed moon with a halo, twinkling stars, a rare shooting star). It follows the visitor's day/night booking preference, or the local time of day until they choose (the pre-paint `skyBootScript` in `layout.tsx` sets `data-sky` first: no flash).
- ONE number drives it all: the phase, 0 = day … 1 = night (`sky-engine.ts`). A change animates it over `SKY.transitionMs` (day → sunset → twilight → night, or back as a sunrise); every layer reads it. Colours and tunables: `src/content/sky.ts` (keyframes mixed in OKLab, `sky-color.ts`).
- Cost rules: during a change, only OPACITY animates in the DOM (the four gradient keyframes, the sun's white and warm versions, the moon's halo and the stars are stacked static layers crossfaded on the compositor); per-frame CSS variables go only to the sky layer and `[data-sky-follow]` elements, NEVER to html (that restyles the whole page every frame). The page's theme flips once, half-way, inside a View Transition (a GPU crossfade of two snapshots; instant without it or with reduced motion). The clouds (`sky-clouds.ts`: 3 parallax layers of domain-warped noise, lit from the sun/moon) are the only per-frame drawing: a fraction of the resolution, capped at `SKY.cloudMaxPixels` (big screens never cost more), 30 fps when idle, full rate while scrolling or changing, nothing while the tunnel scene covers the sky (IntersectionObserver on `#main`) or the tab is hidden; sideways through the world the clouds drift sideways (the shader's `uScroll` is [x, y], x from the position in the current lap of the loop: it starts again where the tunnel's stage covers the sky, so it never jumps on screen nor grows too big for float precision). Stars and the moon are painted once (`sky-paint.ts`; the stars only when night first arrives). No WebGL, no high-precision floats or a lost context: the gradient sky without clouds, never an error. Reduced motion: the sun and moon stay put (they fade, never travel), no drift, parallax, twinkle or shooting stars; a short change.
- Choosing: clicking the sun or the moon on the bare sky (never through content: the engine's hit test skips anything inside panels, text, media or controls), the header's compact switch (in the phone menu drawer below `sm`), or the "Day or night?" section's picker (`PeriodControl`, a real radiogroup). All write `period` to `src/lib/booking-store.ts`: ONE merged record (`smokeys:booking` in localStorage) that the booking form will extend; a change never erases other fields. The booking panel and the Contact scene pre-fill the text/email with it and everything else the visitor filled in. A preference is never availability: the visitor still picks the date and time.
- The bus photo's panel follows the sky (`data-sky-follow`): its own sky gradient, warm sunlight on the bus by day, a night grade with the headlights and roof markers glowing at night (masked to the cut-out's outline; light positions in `BusLight`, measured on `src/assets/intro/bus-exterior.webp`).
- Check with `npm run test:sky` (13 checks: what's drawn through each transition, clicking the sky, the booking merge, reduced motion, measured contrast day and night, frame rate, no-JS, and a "Meet the bus" hotspot tapped on a phone; `--quick` skips the 26 s shooting-star wait) against a production build.

## Intro assets pipeline
Despite the name, these are the bus photos ("Meet the bus", via `src/content/assets.ts`), not the intro: the film has its own pipeline (`npm run video:build`). Owner originals live untouched in `assets/intro-source/`. `npm run assets:intro` (`scripts/intro-assets/build.mjs`; macOS 14+, needs `swiftc`) regenerates the two images in `src/assets/intro/`: `bus-exterior.webp` (Apple Vision subject cutout, street removed, 2x Lanczos from the 560px source) and `bus-interior.jpg` (copied untouched, or uprighted and metadata-stripped if it's a camera photo). Replace a source file and re-run; never hand-edit generated files. If a replacement photo changes framing, re-measure the hotspot positions in `src/content/bus.ts` and the night light positions (headlights, roof markers) in `BusLight` (`src/components/home/meet-the-bus.tsx`).
