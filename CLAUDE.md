# Smokey's Party Bus website

Marketing site + lead capture for Smokey's, a black party bus brand. Plan and decisions: `docs/architecture.md`.

## Stack
Next.js 16.3 (App Router, all routes static) · React 19.3 · TypeScript 6 strict (typescript-eslint caps TS < 6.1) · Tailwind 4 (tokens in `src/app/globals.css`) · Motion 13 for normal UI (`motion/react`, `m` components under `LazyMotion` in `src/components/providers.tsx`) · GSAP 3 for the intro only · the living sky behind every page is a small hand-written WebGL shader plus DOM layers (no Three.js).

## Local tooling
Node 24 lives at `~/.local/node/bin` (not on the default PATH). Prefix commands:
`export PATH=~/.local/node/bin:$PATH` then `npm run dev | build | lint | typecheck`.

## Conventions
- Content lives in `src/content/*.ts`. Anything unconfirmed has `sample: true` / `placeholder: true` and renders a visible `<SampleTag>`. Never present placeholder art or specs as the real bus.
- All media goes through `src/content/assets.ts` slots; `src: null` renders the illustrated placeholder.
- The intro film is the owner's own AI render. It carries the generator's small "Ai" badge (top left of the frame): visible on desktop and partly on phones with a toolbar; never removed without the owner's say-so. Its bus is a generic coach, not Smokey's: keep `introFilm.placeholder` (the "Placeholder footage" tag, shown in every layout) until EVERY built cut shows the real bus. Never add Warner Bros. material (the cartoon roadrunner, its name, sounds or artwork) and never use third-party footage (e.g. TikTok downloads) in the deployed site: the owner provides licensed or own footage.
- Server Components by default; `"use client"` only on interactive leaves. The intro's layers are server-rendered slots passed into the client `IntroSequence`.
- Motion values come from `src/lib/motion.ts` (mirrors CSS tokens). Honour reduced motion: `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`; the intro engine has its own stills-only timeline.
- Nav links are homepage anchors ("/#bus", so they work from any route) until each page gets its own route (`src/content/site.ts`).
- Two themes, switched by the sky: `html[data-sky="day"]` (white glass, deep navy text) and night (navy glass, near-white text; also the no-JS default). Components use only the tokens (`bg-surface`, `bg-surface-raised`, `bg-muted`, `ring-border`, `text-muted-foreground`, `text-gold` for gold TEXT, `bg-primary` for gold fills, `bg-ink` for black-and-gold): never `white/x`, `black` or neon in UI (the neons are only for the party illustrations). Anything that always sits on dark media (the intro stage, the gallery lightbox) gets `.theme-night`. Sections are glass panels (`glass` utility + `data-panel`) floating over the sky; no section paints a solid background.

## Intro sequence (src/components/intro)
A scroll-driven film in one pinned stage: ONE continuous shot (the roadrunner runs into a tunnel → the dust swallows him → the black party bus drives out of it) scrubbed by the scroll → SMOKEY'S → CTAs → the stage unpins and the page continues. Scrolling back up plays it in reverse.
- The FILM provides all the movement (`src/content/intro-video.ts`: the one place for its processing, framing and story beats, merged with the build's `intro-video.generated.json` in `intro-film.ts`). Two cuts of the same shot, each from its own master: `portrait` (phones, tall screens) and `wide` (16:9: desktops, landscape). Each screen gets the closer cut; while one has no master the other stands in (a wide screen shows the portrait cut as a feathered band over a pre-blurred glow of the same scene). Web files in `public/video/intro/` are BUILT, never hand-made: masters in `assets/intro-source/video/` (git-ignored; see its README) → `npm run video:build`. A new render only needs its own `beats` (tunnel, swallowed, cover, headlights, rest: seconds after trim.start, read off `npm run video:inspect -- <master> --from= --to= --step=` strips; the build ends the film just after `rest`).
- `intro-engine.ts`: ONE GSAP master timeline, scrubbed by ScrollTrigger over a tall section (`.intro-scroll`, `INTRO.scroll.screens` screen-heights) whose stage is pinned with CSS sticky. Timeline units = scroll, not seconds (`intro-config.ts`): each stretch between the film's beats gets its own share of the scroll (the dust, where he becomes the bus, gets extra). The film NEVER plays: it's seeked frame-accurately to the playhead (encoded at ≤30 fps, a keyframe every 4 frames, no B-frames), one seek in flight, so autoplay rules don't apply. GSAP adds the camera (a push, a charge into the dust, settling on the bus), a dust haze at the swap, the glow crossfades, the caption, branding and CTAs. Film and header state are derived in one idempotent `sync()` from the playhead, so any scroll jump in either direction lands on the right frame; sync() ignores ScrollTrigger's revert during a refresh and re-runs after it.
- Scrubbed-timeline rules: every tween is a fromTo with explicit start AND end, later tweens use `immediateRender: false`, and each element's CSS resting state equals its first tween's start (ScrollTrigger reverts the timeline when it re-measures). Never call ScrollTrigger.refresh() while the page is scrolling (it restores the scroll position it measured at).
- `intro-media.ts`: picks the cut and file for the screen (phone-sized → the lighter `-small` file; H.264 first, VP9 next), locks in that cut's framing, stills and glows (turning a phone never swaps them), downloads the film COMPLETELY to a blob URL and primes its decoder before the scroll unlocks (no stalls or black frames). `intro-sequence.tsx`: life cycle only (branded loader until the opening still is on screen, then "Loading footage…" with progress while the page waits at the top, Skip), zero React renders while scrolling. If the film isn't in memory within `INTRO.filmTimeoutMs`, the stills tell the story.
- Framing: the film sits in `.intro-film` (globals.css) with its cut's own aspect ratio: covers the frame (the small viewport; the stage itself is 100lvh so the film fills a phone once its toolbar hides) like object-fit cover, never cropping more than `1 − minVisible` of its width or height; past that it becomes a feathered band (`data-banded` y/x) over the glow. Tall/wide framing is chosen in CSS (first paint is right without JS). Never stretch footage.
- 60fps rules: transforms and opacity only; nothing created mid-scroll; the glows are pre-blurred at build time (no live CSS blur). Check with `npm run perf:intro -- --gpu --frames` (page fps and film frames shown per second) and `npm run test:intro` (exits 1 on any uncaught page error) against a production build.
- Accessibility: reduced motion or Save-Data = the same story as crossfaded stills (start, dust, rest) on a shorter scroll, no film downloaded. The H1 stays in the accessibility tree throughout (opacity only); the CTAs are invisible (out of the Tab order) until they appear. Skip jumps to the end and focuses the headline (also before the page's JS has run: the boot script in `layout.tsx`). No JavaScript (or the engine fails): the finished hero on the film's resting frame, no scroll story.

## Living sky (src/components/sky)
Behind every page (`LivingSky` in the root layout; `body` paints no background, `html` does, under it): day (blue sky, sun with soft rays, white clouds) or night (navy sky, detailed moon with a halo, twinkling stars, a rare shooting star). It follows the visitor's day/night booking preference, or the local time of day until they choose (the pre-paint `skyBootScript` in `layout.tsx` sets `data-sky` first: no flash).
- ONE number drives it all: the phase, 0 = day … 1 = night (`sky-engine.ts`). A change animates it over `SKY.transitionMs` (day → sunset → twilight → night, or back as a sunrise); every layer reads it. Colours and tunables: `src/content/sky.ts` (keyframes mixed in OKLab, `sky-color.ts`).
- Cost rules: during a change, only OPACITY animates in the DOM (the four gradient keyframes, the sun's white and warm versions, the moon's halo and the stars are stacked static layers crossfaded on the compositor); per-frame CSS variables go only to the sky layer and `[data-sky-follow]` elements, NEVER to html (that restyles the whole page every frame). The page's theme flips once, half-way, inside a View Transition (a GPU crossfade of two snapshots; instant without it or with reduced motion). The clouds (`sky-clouds.ts`: 3 parallax layers of domain-warped noise, lit from the sun/moon) are the only per-frame drawing: a fraction of the resolution, 30 fps when idle, full rate while scrolling or changing, nothing while the intro covers the sky (IntersectionObserver on `#main`) or the tab is hidden. Stars and the moon are painted once (`sky-paint.ts`). Reduced motion: no drift, parallax, twinkle or shooting stars; a short change.
- Choosing: clicking the sun or the moon on the bare sky (never through content: the engine's hit test skips anything inside panels, text, media or controls), the header's compact switch (in the phone menu drawer below `sm`), or the "Day or night?" section's picker (`PeriodControl`, a real radiogroup). All write `period` to `src/lib/booking-store.ts`: ONE merged record (`smokeys:booking` in localStorage) that the booking form will extend; a change never erases other fields. The quote section shows it and pre-fills the text/email with it. A preference is never availability: the visitor still picks the date and time.
- The bus photo's panel follows the sky (`data-sky-follow`): its own sky gradient, warm sunlight on the bus by day, a night grade with the headlights and roof markers glowing at night (masked to the cut-out's outline; light positions measured on `src/assets/intro/bus-exterior.webp`).
- Check with `npm run test:sky` (11 checks incl. contrast and frame rate; `--quick` skips the 26 s shooting-star wait) against a production build.

## Intro assets pipeline
Owner originals live untouched in `assets/intro-source/`. `node scripts/intro-assets/build.mjs` (macOS 14+, needs `swiftc`) regenerates everything in `src/assets/intro/`: Apple Vision subject cutouts, the subject-free motion-blurred loop plates, 2x Lanczos versions of the low-res sources, the interior photo and the dust sprites. Replace a source file and re-run; never hand-edit generated files. If a replacement photo changes framing, re-measure `assets.intro.bus.headlights` and the hotspot positions in `src/content/bus.ts`.
